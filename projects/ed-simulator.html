Emergency Department Simulator

Discrete-event simulation of patient flow, trolleys and beds. A week of emergency department arrivals simulated minute by minute, with triage priority, a fast-track stream and ward-bed blocking.

Arrival pattern, triage mix, treatment times and admission rates are illustrative assumptions, not data from any hospital. The point is the behaviour: delays come from beds as much as from doctors.

Question

Why do emergency departments clog up?

Irish emergency departments are measured on how many patients leave within a set number of hours, and trolley counts are reported every morning. This simulator lets you test the usual explanations yourself.

  • Add doctors and the wait to be seen falls quickly, but trolley numbers barely move.
  • Free more ward beds and trolleys clear, which also frees cubicles and staff.
  • Turn off fast-track and minor cases queue behind urgent ones.
Method

Discrete-event simulation in the browser

Arrivals follow a non-homogeneous Poisson process with a daily curve. Each patient gets a Manchester-style triage category, waits in a priority queue for a clinician, receives a log-normal treatment time and is admitted with a category-specific probability. Admitted patients wait for ward beds released through the day, first come first served.

The first day is discarded as warm-up, and every run uses the same random seed so changes you see come from the levers, not from noise.
