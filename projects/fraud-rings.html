Fraud Ring Detector

Finding coordinated account rings in a network of shared devices and cards. Graph analytics on a synthetic account network with planted abuse rings and innocent families who share a device.

Accounts are linked when they share a device (cyan), a payment card (amber) or an IP block (grey). The detector groups linked accounts and scores each group. Raise or lower the threshold, then select any account to see why it was or was not flagged.

Synthetic data with known answers, so the detector can be scored honestly. Real investigations add many more signals and a human review step before any action.

Question

Can you tell a fraud ring from a family?

Both share devices and addresses. What separates them is behaviour: rings sign up in bursts, reuse a few cards across many accounts, transact heavily and attract chargebacks. Families sign up over weeks and behave normally.

  • Connected components find clusters of linked accounts.
  • Each cluster is scored on size, signup burst, card reuse, activity and chargebacks.
  • Precision and recall update live, so you can see the cost of a lower threshold: more rings caught, more families wrongly flagged.
Why I built it

The same judgement as my Trust and Safety work

In my day job I review high volumes of cases and look for patterns that suggest coordinated activity. This lab turns that judgement into an explicit, measurable method, with the trade-off between catching abuse and protecting innocent users in plain view.

Skills: graph construction, union-find clustering, feature design, risk scoring, threshold tuning, precision and recall, force-directed layout on canvas.
